# Integrating Large Language Models into Security Incident Response

## Bibliographic Information

Citation: Kramer, D. et al. (2025). Integrating Large Language Models into Security Incident Response. Twenty-First Symposium on Usable Privacy and Security (SOUPS 2025).

Status: Read 

## Research Aim

* Test LLMs for autonomous incident summarisation.
* Compare AI, human and AI-assisted summaries.
* Assess benefits and risks of LLM assistance.

## Methodology

* Used 50 real-world security incidents.
* Involved 18 Google security analysts.
* Tested Gemini 1.5 Flash.
* Covered five common incident types.
* Compared summary quality and analyst effort.

## Key Findings

* Human summaries were preferred 61% over autonomous AI.
* AI omitted critical details in 35% of cases.
* AI introduced factual errors in 42% of cases.
* AI-assisted summaries were preferred 77% of the time.
* Human oversight significantly improved results.

## Strengths

* Uses real-world security incidents.
* Uses experienced security analysts.
* Tests autonomous vs assisted LLM use.
* Evaluates factuality and completeness.

## Limitations Relevant to My Scope

* Focuses only on final incident summarisation.
* Does not test full autonomous investigation.
* Uses only Gemini 1.5 Flash.
* Incident data is Google-specific.

## Relevance to My Project

* Directly questions autonomous LLM reliability.
* Highlights hallucination and omission risks.
* Supports evaluating accuracy and completeness.
* Shows why evidence-supported conclusions matter.

## Methodology I Could Adapt

* Use known incident ground truth.
* Score factual accuracy and completeness.
* Record hallucinations and unsupported claims.
* Use consistent structured prompts.

## Research Gap

* Full incident investigation is not tested.
* LLM receives completed investigation details.
* Autonomous evidence interpretation remains unexplored.
* More advanced LLMs and grounding need testing.
