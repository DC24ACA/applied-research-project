# Managing the Human-AI Collaboration in Cloud Security Operations Centers (SOCs)

## Bibliographic Information

Citation: Bansal, S., Bhariya, O. P., Sharma, D., Battu, D., Shamila, M. and Monika, S. (2025). Managing the Human-AI Collaboration in Cloud Security Operations Centers (SOCs). 2025 IEEE International Conference on Innovate for Humanitarian: Tech Solutions for Global Challenge (ICIH). DOI: 10.1109/ICIH67754.2025.11608672.

Supporting Literature

Status: Read 

## Research Aim

* Improve collaboration between AI systems and human SOC analysts.
* Balance automation with human judgement, trust and oversight.
* Improve threat detection and incident response in cloud SOCs.

## Methodology

* Proposed a hybrid Human-AI SOC framework.
* Dynamically allocated tasks between AI, humans or both depending on confidence and complexity.
* Included explainable AI, trust scoring and continuous human feedback.

## Key Findings

* Proposed framework achieved 96.2% accuracy and 94.6% precision.
* Reported a 24.9-second average response time compared with 58.6 seconds for manual analysts.
* Human-AI collaboration achieved higher trust scores than AI-only approaches. 

## Strengths

* Directly examines AI use within a SOC.
* Considers trust, explainability and human oversight.
* Compares human-only, AI-only and collaborative approaches.

## Limitations Relevant to My Scope

* Focuses on Human-AI collaboration rather than autonomous AI.
* Does not specifically use LLMs, Wazuh or Sysmon.
* Experimental/simulation details are limited, so reported results should be treated cautiously.

## Relevance to My Project

* Provides evidence for why autonomous AI outputs need careful evaluation.
* Highlights trust and explainability as important issues when AI makes security decisions.

## Methodology I Could Adapt

* Clearly define the level of autonomy given to the AI.
* Evaluate whether AI conclusions are explainable and supported by evidence.
* Consider where human verification would still be required.

## Research Gap

* Does not test fully autonomous LLM incident investigation.
* Does not evaluate AI against known incident ground truth.
* Does not assess whether an LLM can independently reconstruct an incident from security evidence.
