# AI-Driven Security Operations Centers: A Research Landscape Analysis

## Bibliographic Information

Citation: Adem, A. İ., Bakal, M. F. and Sezen, A. (2026). AI-Driven Security Operations Centers: A Research Landscape Analysis. 2026 4th Cognitive Models and Artificial Intelligence Conference (AICCONF). DOI: 10.1109/AICCONF69182.2026.11600709.

Priority: Core Literature

Status: Read

## Research Aim

- Map how AI and LLMs are used in SOCs.
- Identify current levels of SOC automation.
- Examine the models and architectures being used.
- Review how AI-enabled SOC systems are evaluated.
- Identify risks, limitations and human oversight needs.

## Methodology

- Conducted a systematic mapping study.
- Searched Google Scholar using two search strings.
- Examined 200 studies published from 2023 to 2025.
- Applied inclusion and exclusion criteria.
- Selected and analysed 44 peer-reviewed studies.

## Key Findings

- AI is mainly used for detection, triage and analyst support.
- Most systems are human-in-the-loop or semi-autonomous.
- Fully autonomous SOCs remain mainly conceptual.
- Common metrics include precision, recall, detection time and response time.
- Hallucinations and over-reliance remain important risks.

## Strengths

- Used a structured systematic mapping method.
- Included clear research questions.
- Applied defined selection criteria.
- Covered several areas of AI-enabled SOC research.
- Examined both performance and human involvement.

## Limitations Relevant to My Scope

- Used only Google Scholar.
- Examined only the first ten pages of results.
- Covered a limited period from 2023 to 2025.
- Did not conduct a practical experiment.
- Did not directly evaluate Wazuh or Sysmon investigations.

## Relevance to My Project

- Provides an overview of AI use in SOCs.
- Supports investigating autonomous LLM limitations.
- Shows that human verification remains common.
- Identifies possible evaluation metrics.
- Helps position my project within current research.

## Methodology I Could Adapt

- Define clear research and evaluation questions.
- Measure technical and operational performance.
- Evaluate hallucinations and unsupported conclusions.
- Record findings in a structured comparison matrix.

## Research Gap

- Fully autonomous SOCs lack practical testing.
- Few studies examine complete incident investigations.
- Evaluation methods are not standardised.
- Investigation from endpoint evidence remains underexplored.
- Comparisons between different LLM types remain limited.
