* growing use of LLMs within SOC environments for analysing alerts, logs and incident data.
* most current research focuses on LLMs as tools that support analysts rather than fully replace them.
* Kramer et al. (2025) – LLMs were used across real-world security incidents and were most effective when working alongside human analysts.
* Cadet et al. (2026) – demonstrated that LLM-based approaches can analyse different types of security evidence and help reconstruct incident activity.
* Eilertsen et al. (2026) – shows movement towards more agentic and automated security investigation workflows.
* studies suggest that LLMs can contribute to investigation tasks but they differ in how much independence is given to the model.
* limitations include: inaccurate conclusions, missing events and difficulty maintaining evidence-based reasoning.
* the literature demonstrates that LLMs have value in SOC investigations, but their ability to complete an investigation autonomously is still less clearly established.
* this creates a clear research gap around whether LLMs can independently conduct complete and reliable security incident investigations without human guidance.
